# Odisha Disaster Management Context & Operational Baseline

## 1. Geographic & Hazard Profile of Odisha
Odisha has an 480-kilometer coastline along the Bay of Bengal and is historically one of the most disaster-prone regions in South Asia. Over the past 120 years, Odisha has been struck by over 100 severe cyclonic storms, annual riverine floods, severe summer heatwaves, and seasonal landslides.

### 1.1. Coastal Cyclone & Storm Surge Belt (6 High-Risk Districts)
1. **Puri**: Vulnerable to direct landfall (e.g., Cyclone Fani, May 2019, 215 km/h winds). Chilika lake surge threat.
2. **Jagatsinghpur**: Site of the devastating 1999 Super Cyclone (landfall at Ersama, 260+ km/h, 7-meter storm surge).
3. **Kendrapara**: Low-lying mangrove estuaries (Bhitarkanika), Brahmani-Mahanadi delta mouth.
4. **Bhadrak**: Dhamra port area, Baitarani river flood backflow.
5. **Balasore**: Coastal tidal surges combined with Subarnarekha and Budhabalanga river spates.
6. **Ganjam**: Gopalpur coast (Cyclone Phailin 2013 landfall, Cyclone Titli 2018).

### 1.2. Major River Basins & Flood Inundation Systems
- **Mahanadi River Basin**: Regulated by the **Hirakud Dam** (Sambalpur). High inflow from upstream Chhattisgarh leads to emergency sluice gate openings. Peak discharge causes catastrophic flooding at Mundali Barrage (Cuttack), spilling into Cuttack, Jagatsinghpur, Kendrapara, and Puri.
- **Brahmani River Basin**: Regulated by Rengali Dam, impacting Angul, Dhenkanal, Jajpur, and Kendrapara.
- **Baitarani River Basin**: Non-dammed basin, critical flood gauge at **Akhuapada**, frequently flooding Jajpur and Bhadrak districts.
- **Subarnarekha River Basin**: Flooding in northern Balasore (Jaleswar, Bhograi blocks).
- **Rushikulya River Basin**: Flooding in Ganjam district.

### 1.3. Extreme Heatwave Zone (Interior Odisha)
- Western Odisha districts: **Bolangir, Titlagarh, Kalahandi, Nuapada, Sambalpur, Jharsuguda, Bargarh**. Summer temperatures frequently exceed 45°C–47°C, requiring water kiosk monitoring, heatstroke hospitalization tracking, and midday work restrictions.

## 3. Physical & Tactical Assets

### 3.1. Multi-Purpose Cyclone & Flood Shelters (MCS / MFS)
- Over 800 engineered cyclone shelters constructed across the 0–10 km coastal belt.
- Built to withstand 250+ km/h wind loads and elevated on stilts above highest historical storm surge levels.
- Designed with separate dormitories for women and men, dedicated water storage cisterns, rooftop helipad markings, and diesel generator rooms.
- Governed locally by Cyclone Shelter Management and Maintenance Committees (CSMMC).

### 3.2. Tactical Emergency Response Units
- **ODRAF (Odisha Disaster Rapid Action Force)**: Specialized state force trained for swift-water rescue, collapsed structure search and rescue (CSSR), and high-wind clearance.
- **NDRF 3rd Battalion (Mundali, Cuttack)**: Federal tactical response prepositioned across coastal belts during severe weather alerts.
- **Odisha Fire & Emergency Services (OFES)**: 335+ fire stations with inflatable motorized rescue boats (BAUTs), tower lights, and deep-well pumps.
